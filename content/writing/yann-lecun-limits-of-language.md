Yann LeCun has been, by far, one of the most interesting people to read about out of all those at the forefront of AI R&D. I recently watched an interesting video by TheAIGrid which detailed a recent groundbreaking paper that covered many things from Yann LeCun, including a paper he and Randall Balestriero recently published that turned the Platonic Representation Hypothesis into a formal theorem. The hypothesis, in layman's terms, was created from the observations of AI researchers, and asserts that independently trained deep neural networks, regardless of differentiations in architecture, or objectives, are converging toward a shared statistical model of reality.

LeCun also spoke to how we should be viewing the potential that LLMs actually carry. He talked about how LLMs are trained: tokens representing groupings or, more often, individual words are used for models to learn and predict what comes next. He explains how there are two limitations in this: a data wall, and a weird dichotomy between the application of LLMs in theoretical/abstract vs real-world tasks. Companies like Meta, OpenAI and Anthropic have spent the last couple years developing LLMs and using text based data available to the public online. Fable 5.2, released less than a week ago by Anthropic, is reportedly estimated to have been trained on ~250 trillion tokens. However, I have many questions related to the longevity of this approach for a couple of reasons. <mark>The first being that textual data is limited, and beyond that, text generated by humans is also becoming more scarce.</mark> In 2024 Epoch AI published an article that raised concerns over the relationship between a positively exponential AI R&D boom centred around LLMs and the limited ~300 trillion tokens of high-quality human generated text data that exists currently on the internet, including books and web pages. This study by Epoch says that this supply of high-quality data could run out sometime between 2026 and 2032. We are in 2026 so I guess we’ll see what happens. Running out leaves a massive risk for the phenomenon of Model Collapse to occur, where AI models trained on AI-generated data effectively start “eating their own exhaust”. Appen, citing an earlier Epoch study, also notes that the current reserve of low-quality human generated text and image data could run dry anytime between the years 2030 and 2060.

<mark>Clearly, the unregulated, and flash deployment and introduction of generative AI into everyday life has not only created developmental issues in people, but also in the capacity of their parent companies to make them better.</mark> If I was Sam Altman, Dario Amodei or Mark Zuckerberg, I would be lobbying the world’s media giants (South China Morning Post, The Guardian, NY Times), education boards, and whoever else to encourage them to NOT use AI, to ensure that my researchers and engineers had a continuous stream of high-quality human generated data. But, let me digress.

What I understood from Yann LeCun is that he thinks that the majority of AI companies are doing the wrong thing by continuing and accelerating this LLM model race. He made a very interesting comparison between textual data and visual data (photos, videos etc.). The amount of data currently available on the internet in text form would take the average person ~100,000 years to read through in full. But a human child, after about four years of life, has taken in the same amount of data in audio/visual form as these massive AI models have taken in from all the text on the internet, without even knowing language. This is a very interesting perspective to look at the strengths and weaknesses of text-based data in comparison to audio/visual data, and it has reminded me of the limitations that language has not only in AI but also in creativity and expression.

I will be applying a lot of his thoughts on the future AI to how I plan future projects and what skills I look to dive into acquiring. I encourage everyone to keep an eye on what Yann LeCun is saying, doing, and publishing–truly an extraordinary thinker.
